# Multiple Choice Questions

## Question 1
What was the main lesson the Buddha wanted Kisa Gotami to learn by asking her to collect mustard seeds from a house where no one had lost a loved one?

A. That mustard seeds have healing powers  
B. That death and suffering are universal  
C. That she should pray for her son  
D. That she should leave her village  

**Answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The Buddha wanted Kisa Gotami to realize that death is a universal experience and not unique to her, helping her come to terms with her grief.

## Question 2
According to the Buddha’s sermon, what is the consequence of weeping and grieving excessively for the dead?

A. The dead will return  
B. It brings peace of mind  
C. It increases one’s own suffering  
D. It saves the living from death  

**Answer:** C  
**Explanation:** The Buddha taught that excessive lamentation only increases personal suffering and does not benefit the dead.

## Question 3
How did Kisa Gotami’s understanding of grief change after her encounter with the Buddha?

A. She believed she could bring her son back  
B. She realized her grief was unique  
C. She understood that everyone experiences loss  
D. She became angry at her neighbors  

**Answer:** C  
**Explanation:** After visiting many houses, Kisa Gotami grasped that grief is a common human experience, not something unique to her alone.

A. An entertaining story

## Question 5
Why does the Buddha compare mortals to ripe fruits and earthen vessels?

A. To show that life is full of sweetness  
B. To illustrate the inevitability of death  
C. To praise the beauty of nature  
D. To warn against eating fruits  

**Answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The Buddha uses these comparisons to highlight that just as ripe fruits fall and earthen pots break, all living beings are bound to die.

## Question 6
In the passage, what does the phrase “draw out the arrow of lamentation” mean?

A. To ignore one’s problems  
B. To remove the cause of sorrow and grief  
C. To attack those who cause pain  
D. To cry loudly  

**Answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The phrase is a metaphor suggesting that one should remove the source of their sorrow to find peace of mind.

## Question 7
What is one major difference between the Buddha’s approach to grief and the modern perspectives described in “A Guide to Coping with the Death of a Loved One”?

A. The Buddha discourages talking about feelings  
B. Modern guides recognize a variety of emotional responses  
C. The Buddha encourages ignoring all emotions  
D. Modern guides say grief should always be hidden  

**Answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Modern perspectives acknowledge that grief is unique and can involve multiple emotions, while the Buddha’s approach is more focused on acceptance and detachment.

## Question 8
According to the text, what does Amitai Etzioni argue about the “right” way to grieve?

A. There is only one correct way to grieve  
B. Grief should always follow specific stages  
C. There is no set form for grief; everyone grieves differently  
D. Grief should not be expressed at all  

**Answer:** C
