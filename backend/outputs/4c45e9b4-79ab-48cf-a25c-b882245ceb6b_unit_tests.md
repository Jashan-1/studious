# Unit Test Questions

## Question 1
When her son dies, Kisa Gotami goes from house to house. What does she ask for? Does she get it? Why not?

**Model Answer:**  
Kisa Gotami asks for medicine to cure her dead son, believing that there is a way to bring him back to life. However, she does not receive the medicine she seeks because the people she approaches understand that her son is dead and cannot be revived, which emphasizes the universal reality of death that she fails to grasp at that moment.

**Key Points:**
- Kisa Gotami's request for medicine reflects her denial of death.
- She does not receive the medicine because death is irreversible.
- The interaction highlights her initial inability to accept the loss.

## Question 2
Kisa Gotami again goes from house to house after she speaks with the Buddha. What does she ask for, the second time around? Does she get it? Why not?

**Model Answer:**  
The second time, Kisa Gotami asks for mustard seeds from a household that has not experienced death. She does not get the mustard seeds because every household she visits has suffered the loss of a loved one, illustrating the universality of grief and loss, which she finally begins to understand.

**Key Points:**
- Kisa Gotami seeks mustard seeds as a condition set by the Buddha.
- She learns that every household has experienced death.
- This realization leads her to understand that grief is a common human experience.

## Question 3
What does Kisa Gotami understand the second time that she failed to understand the first time? Was this what the Buddha wanted her to understand?

**Model Answer:**  
Kisa Gotami realizes that grief and loss are universal experiences shared by all people, not just her alone. This understanding aligns with the Buddha's intention to help her recognize that her sorrow is part of the human condition, encouraging her to let go of her selfish grief.

**Key Points:**
- She realizes the universality of grief.
- This understanding contrasts with her earlier belief of being uniquely afflicted.
- The Buddha aimed for her to find peace through this realization.

## Question 4
Why do you think Kisa Gotami understood this only the second time? In what way did the Buddha change her understanding?

**Model Answer:**  
Kisa Gotami's understanding deepens the second time because her experience of searching for mustard seeds forces her to confront the reality of death in others' lives. The Buddha changes her perspective from a personal, isolated grief to a broader understanding of shared human suffering, prompting her to reflect on the nature of life and death.

**Key Points:**
- Her journey for mustard seeds serves as a catalyst for understanding.
- The Buddha’s teaching encourages reflection on collective suffering.
- She shifts from selfish grief to recognizing shared mortality.

## Question 5
How do you usually understand the idea of ‘selfishness’? Do you agree with Kisa Gotami that she was being ‘selfish in her grief’?

**Model Answer:**  
Selfishness is often understood as prioritizing one's own needs and desires over those of others. In Kisa Gotami's case, her intense focus on her personal grief blinded her to the suffering of others. I agree with her realization that her grief was selfish because it isolated her from the shared human experience of loss, which ultimately hindered her healing process.

**Key Points:**
- Selfishness involves self-centeredness at the expense of others.
- Kisa Gotami's grief initially prevents her from seeing others' pain.
- Her eventual understanding highlights the importance of empathy in grief.
